About Saans & Methodology

How Saans calculates safe daylight hours, indoor purifier sizing, and cleaner commute swaps for families living through winter smog.

1. What Saans is

Saans (the Hindi word for breath) is an urban air quality assistant designed to help families navigate severe seasonal smog. Instead of stopping at a single color-coded air quality index number, Saans translates ambient PM2.5 forecasts into hour-by-hour schedules, room-specific purifier guidelines, and low-emission commute alternatives tailored to who lives in your home.

2. The winter smog problem

During winter months across northern India and similar continental basins, thermal inversions trap cold air near the ground. Industrial emissions, vehicular exhaust, and biomass burning accumulate beneath this stagnant layer, creating prolonged spikes in fine particulate matter (PM2.5).

Particles smaller than 2.5 micrometers penetrate deep into the lungs and enter the bloodstream. Because atmospheric conditions change dramatically between nighttime inversion peaks and afternoon solar heating, timing daily activities can reduce exposure.

4. Where air data comes from

Saans queries the Open-Meteo Air Quality API, which assimilates global and regional chemical transport models, including ECMWF Copernicus Atmosphere Monitoring Service (CAMS) and Finnish Meteorological Institute (SILAM).

These values represent continuous atmospheric model estimates averaged over grid cells. They do not rely on a single physical roadside monitor that might suffer downtime or local bias. However, because regional models can differ from localized hyper-local sensors, users can provide their own manual PM2.5 readings at any time.

5. Air Quality Index (AQI) Chart & Calculation

Saans uses the official Central Pollution Control Board (CPCB) National Air Quality Index (NAQI) framework. While raw particulate concentrations in µg/m³ can be difficult to interpret across pollutants, the standardized 0–500 AQI scale translates complex atmospheric chemistry into direct, actionable health guidance.

How AQI is Calculated (CPCB NAQI Formula)

The Central Pollution Control Board (CPCB) computes the National Air Quality Index using piecewise linear interpolation between discrete concentration breakpoints. The formula maps any raw pollutant concentration \(C\) into an indexed sub-index score \(I\):

AQI = I_low + [ (I_high - I_low) / (C_high - C_low) ] × (C - C_low)

• C: Raw observed pollutant concentration (PM2.5 in µg/m³).

• C_low, C_high: Concentration category breakpoints enclosing concentration C.

• I_low, I_high: Index breakpoints corresponding to C_low and C_high.

Step-by-Step Worked Example: Delhi Smog Reading (180 µg/m³)

Suppose a winter reading reports a PM2.5 concentration of 180 µg/m³:

  1. Identify Breakpoints: 180 µg/m³ falls inside the Very Poor category: \(C_{\text{low}} = 121\), \(C_{\text{high}} = 250\), with index boundaries \(I_{\text{low}} = 301\) and \(I_{\text{high}} = 400\).
  2. Compute Differences: \((I_{\text{high}} - I_{\text{low}}) = 400 - 301 = 99\), \((C_{\text{high}} - C_{\text{low}}) = 250 - 121 = 129\).
  3. Interpolate: \(\text{AQI} = 301 + \left(\frac{99}{129}\right) \times (180 - 121) = 301 + (0.7674 \times 59) = 301 + 45.28 = \mathbf{346}\).
  4. Outcome: The resulting score is 346 (Very Poor), which dictates high-grade HEPA indoor filtration and sealed windows.

7. What the AI does and does not do

Our AI integration uses {{ ai_name }} to write conversational explanations of your plan. To prevent hallucinations and misleading figures, every response is passed through a numeric grounding filter.

If the AI generates a number with a unit (such as PM2.5 concentrations, percentages, or distances) that was not part of the deterministic rule output, that statement is automatically removed before you see it. The AI cannot modify your calculated recommendations.

8. Privacy & session storage

Saans requires no user accounts, passwords, or logins.

Your household inputs are held temporarily in your browser's sessionStorage. Nothing is stored in a database or tracked across web sessions. Closing your browser tab clears your inputs.

When looking up live atmospheric data, your city name is sent to Open-Meteo. When using Explain or Ask, a summary of your plan (which can mention sensitive household groups) plus your notes or question is sent to {{ ai_name }}.

9. Limitations & health precautions

Not medical advice

Saans provides general precautions based on environmental air quality estimates. It cannot diagnose or treat medical conditions. Individuals with asthma, COPD, cardiovascular illness, or pregnancy should follow guidance from their physician.

Model estimates may differ from hyper-local sensor readings due to micro-climates, wind shifts, or local fires.
