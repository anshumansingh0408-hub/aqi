Where the air data comes from

Saans retrieves readings from the Open-Meteo Air Quality API, which uses atmospheric chemical transport models (including ECMWF CAMS and SILAM) to estimate regional surface PM2.5 concentrations.

Because these are model-derived estimates rather than micro-local physical sensors, conditions on your street may vary depending on local traffic or burning. You can always provide a manual PM2.5 reading from a local sensor if you prefer.

Frequently asked questions

Is Saans providing medical advice?

No. Saans offers practical environmental guidance and general precautions. Anyone with respiratory or cardiovascular conditions should follow instructions from their doctor.

How accurate are the PM2.5 numbers?

The values are regional atmospheric model estimates. They reflect broad urban background pollution, not specific hyper-local readings right outside your doorstep.

What is CADR and why is it important?

Clean Air Delivery Rate (CADR) measures the volume of filtered air a purifier produces. We compute minimum CADR targeting 4 air changes per hour for your room volume.

Can I use Saans without sharing my city?

Yes. You can enter any city name or provide a manual PM2.5 number without geographic lookups.

Is my family information stored on a server?

No. All requests are stateless. Your household inputs are stored only in your browser session storage and are never saved to a database.
